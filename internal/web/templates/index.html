Find out what people actually want.

QuickSurvey asks one question well: which of these are you interested in? You write the options, share one link, and everyone gives a thumbs-up to as many as they like.

Got a link from someone? Open it directly — there is nothing to sign up for.

Honest about the limits: the application records no IP address, but the web server in front of it keeps ordinary access logs, and on a small survey a timestamp is enough to line those up with a response. Treat this as “nobody is collecting your identity”, not as “nobody could ever work it out”.

No account, no sign-up

Answering asks nothing of you: no name, no email, no password. What gets stored is which options you picked, and a random token from your own browser — nothing that identifies you to us.

One vote per person, gently

A cookie keeps casual double-voting out — not a determined one. Change your mind and re-submit: your new answer replaces the old one instead of stacking on top.

Pick as many as you like

Options are not mutually exclusive. Thumbs-up everything that appeals, ignore the rest. Respondents can suggest options too, subject to a moderator.

Results you can actually use

Download a tab-separated file and drop it straight into Google Sheets — one row per response to pivot, or a ready-made tally.
